# Coding-Agent Evidence Registry

**Registry version:** 2026-08-29

**Last source verification:** 2026-08-29

**Status:** research registry; not a runtime rule set

## Purpose and schema

This registry keeps evidence identity, authority, applicability, contradiction,
and expiry visible. A listed practice is not automatically a recommendation.
Every future entry should preserve these fields:

- stable ID, title, canonical source URL, author/vendor, publication date, and
  last verified date;
- evidence tier and peer-review status;
- model, agent/runtime, task/workload, and population scope;
- claimed benefit, observed outcome, and quantitative effect when reported;
- limitations, independent support, independent contradiction, project
  relevance, confidence, and an expiration/revalidation trigger.

Evidence tiers follow the authorized hierarchy:

1. independent strong empirical or peer-reviewed evidence;
2. vendor research;
3. official product/technical guidance;
4. reproducible technical/community experiment;
5. community experience, useful only for pain discovery and hypotheses.

`Peer-reviewed` means an identified archival venue. arXiv and workshop
preprints remain `preprint` even when their methods appear strong.

### Independent empirical and peer-reviewed evidence

| ID | Source and status | Scope and observed outcome | Project relevance, contradiction, and expiry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IND-001 | [METR, *Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity*](https://metr.org/Early_2025_AI_Experienced_OS_Devs_Study-paper.pdf), 2025-07-10; independent RCT; not identified as peer-reviewed | 16 experienced OSS developers, 246 tasks, early-2025 Cursor/Claude tooling. AI access increased completion time by 19% while participants believed it made them faster. | Strong for perception-versus-measurement and this population; not a universal AI-productivity estimate. Revalidate for later model/tool generations; METR's 2026 follow-up reports severe selection/time-measurement limits. |
| IND-002 | [Liu et al., *Lost in the Middle*](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/), TACL 2024; peer-reviewed | Controlled QA and retrieval across several older open/closed models. Relevant information was used less reliably in the middle of long contexts; more context did not guarantee better use. | Supports selective/progressive context, but is not a coding-agent study and predates current models. Revalidate on each material model generation and coding workload. |
| IND-003 | [Gloaguen et al., *Evaluating AGENTS.md*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988), 2026-02-12; arXiv preprint and ICLR 2026 workshop paper, not identified as archival peer-reviewed | Multiple agents/models on SWE-bench plus 138 CTXbench tasks. Context files generally did not improve success and raised cost roughly 20% or more; they increased exploration/tool use. | Directly relevant and consistent with this project's search/context findings. Contradicted by IND-004 on operational efficiency. Revalidate by agent, model, repository, and instruction content. |
| IND-004 | [Lulla et al., *On the Impact of AGENTS.md Files on the Efficiency of AI Coding Agents*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20404), 2026-01-23; JAWs workshop preprint, DOI placeholder in source | 10 repositories and 124 PRs, with/without AGENTS.md. Reports 28.64% lower median runtime and 16.58% lower output tokens with comparable completion behavior. | First-class contradiction to IND-003. Completion comparability and workshop/preprint status limit causal/product claims. Revalidate after archival publication and independent replication. |
| IND-005 | [Qian and Wexler, *Take it, Leave it, or Fix it*](https://research.google/pubs/take-it-leave-it-or-fix-it-measuring-productivity-and-trust-in-human-ai-collaboration/), IUI 2024; peer-reviewed | Mixed-method study of 76 software engineers using Bard on programming-exam tasks. Effects varied by expertise and question type; evidence included automation complacency and increased novice performance on some solve tasks. | Strong evidence against one universal assistance policy. Product/model and exam-task scope expire rapidly; replicate in repository-level agent work. |
| IND-006 | [Perry et al., *Do Users Write More Insecure Code with AI Assistants?*](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623157), CCS 2023; peer-reviewed | User study using `codex-davinci-002` on security tasks. Participants with AI wrote less secure code and were more likely to believe it secure. | Supports risk-adaptive verification and confidence/correctness separation. Old model and security-task scope prohibit current universal claims; revalidate by model and user expertise. |
| IND-007 | [Shihab et al., *Effects of GitHub Copilot on Computing Students...*](https://icer2025.acm.org/details/icer-2025-papers/18/The-Effects-of-GitHub-Copilot-on-Computing-Students-Programming-Effectiveness-Effic), ICER 2025; peer-reviewed | Controlled mixed-method study, 10 undergraduates, unfamiliar brownfield tasks. Students were 34.9% faster and made 50% more progress with Copilot; stronger students used suggestions more selectively and participants raised understanding/learning concerns. | Contradicts blanket harm and shows learning goals differ from outcome-only goals. Very small student sample; revalidate before applying to non-students or agentic tools. |
| IND-008 | [Chen et al., *Humans or LLMs as the Judge?*](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.474/), EMNLP 2024; peer-reviewed | Thousands of evaluations show both human and LLM judges vulnerable to multiple perturbation/bias classes. | Supports deterministic execution oracles and calibrated human review over a synthetic judge score. Not code-specific; combine with code-judge evidence already recorded in `RESEARCH_NOTES.md`. |
| IND-009 | [Huang et al., *MetaTool*](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/bc12914d66b41b6bfc2d3a5decdb498b-Abstract-Conference.html), ICLR 2024; peer-reviewed | Eight LLMs evaluated on whether and which tools to use; most struggled, including with similar choices and multi-tool selection. | Supports measuring selection and no-tool decisions, not merely exposing more tools. Models and synthetic tasks age quickly. |
| IND-010 | [Farahani and Johansson, *Tool Preferences in Agentic LLMs are Unreliable*](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1060/), EMNLP 2025; peer-reviewed | Controlled tool-description edits changed selection; promotional wording could increase use more than sevenfold without functional change. | Strong warning against treating invocation frequency as tool value. Revalidate descriptions/models; keep deterministic wrappers and outcome checks separate. |
| IND-011 | [CooperBench](https://cooperbench.com/static/pdfs/main.pdf), 2026-01-19; preprint | 652 collaborative coding tasks across 12 libraries and four languages. Current agents performed materially worse when cooperating than one agent doing the same total workload; communication/commitment failures were central. | Direct counterweight to “more agents = better.” Revalidate on new orchestration runtimes and independently partitionable work. |
| IND-012 | [Dialogue SWE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13995), 2026-06-12; preprint | Dialogue-driven repository tasks. Cites high issue underspecification and rare agent clarification; schema-guided dialogue improved strong baselines 3–14%, while stronger coding models were not always better dialogue models. | Supports studying missing information and clarification separately from prompt polish. Simulator and LLM-judge components require human/execution validation. |
| IND-013 | [Bai et al., *How Do AI Agents Spend Your Money?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22750), 2026-04-24; preprint | Eight frontier models on SWE-bench Verified. Same-task token use varied up to 30x; input dominated; higher token use did not imply higher accuracy and often saturated at intermediate cost. | Supports work-per-correct-outcome, cached/fresh separation, and rejection of token minimization as objective. Benchmark/model generation expires quickly. |
| IND-014 | [Suri et al., *Structured Uncertainty guided Clarification*](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2028/), Findings of ACL 2026; peer-reviewed | Controlled ambiguous tool-use tasks. The structured-uncertainty method reported 7–39% higher coverage with 1.5–2.7x fewer questions than baselines. | Strong evidence that clarification policy can be selective; not a repository-coding or human-interruption study. It narrows, but does not close, candidate H. |
| IND-015 | [Xu et al., *The Devil Is in the Interface*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11386), 2026-08-13; preprint, benchmark-based | 11,700 repository-level trajectories across six tool architectures. Interface structure materially changed consistency, file access, steps, and token use. | Raises the novelty bar for generic tool-count/interface experiments. Harness/model effects and preprint status require independent replication. |
| IND-016 | [Repantis et al., *How Many Tools Should an LLM Agent See?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24660), 2026-05-29; preprint, benchmark-based | Adaptive shortlisting exposed about seven of 50 tools while retaining roughly 90% coverage; downstream selection improved when the correct tool was retained. | Supports catalog reduction only with explicit recall accounting. Mostly non-coding tasks and current models limit transfer. |
| IND-017 | [Chakrabarti et al., *Why Does CLAUDE.md Keep Growing?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11095), 2026-08-13; preprint, observational plus controlled synthetic experiment | 247,694 instruction-file lifetimes in 1,867 repositories showed strong prompt growth. A synthetic instruction-following experiment found that removing comments/rationale reduced excess text and improved performance. | Supports content-type and prompt-growth questions, but does not establish repository-level accepted outcomes or path-scoping efficacy. |
| IND-018 | [*Scaling Test-Time Compute for Agentic Coding*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16529), 2026-04-22; preprint, benchmark experiment | Additional test-time compute improved reported SWE-bench and TerminalBench performance for frontier coding agents under the authors' method. | Establishes conditional coding value beyond a simple effort knob. A new effort study needs task interactions, work/cost, and adequate repetitions, not a two-arm replication. |
| IND-019 | [*A Jagged Frontier*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18389), 2026-08-25; preprint, benchmark experiment | Semantics-preserving presentation changes across 54 repository tasks, two scaffolds, and four models changed scores and sometimes rankings. | Direct warning that harness and task presentation can dominate small effects. Raises reproducibility and half-life gates for every candidate. |

## Current contradiction register

| Question | Supporting direction | Contradictory direction | Current interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Do persistent repository instructions help? | IND-004 reports lower median runtime/output tokens; vendors recommend scoped instructions. | IND-003 reports no general success gain and materially higher cost/exploration. | Content, agent, task, and measurement differ. No universal rule; use the smallest scoped content and evaluate against no-file/native defaults. |
| Does AI improve developer productivity? | IND-007 reports faster student brownfield work; DORA reports positive associations; users report useful narrow tasks. | IND-001 found experienced maintainers slower; community reports cleanup/rework; DORA also finds stability risk. | Population, task, lifecycle, and outcome dominate. “AI productivity” is not one estimand. |
| Is more context better? | VEN-006 documents valuable large-context uses and caching. | IND-002 shows position/length fragility; IND-003 and vendor guidance warn of noise/cost. | Relevant context can help; maximum context is not an objective. Measure retrieval/retention and outcome jointly. |
| Are more agents better? | Vendor guidance identifies independent parallel work as a good fit. | IND-011 and VEN-004 document coordination and token overhead. | Parallelism is a conditional workload feature, not a default architecture. |
| Are fewer tokens better? | Lower input/output can reduce latency or cost under known pricing. | IND-013 shows high variability and non-monotonic accuracy; cached pricing and quality can break the equivalence. | Tokens are one work/cost component. Optimize accepted outcomes, not raw count. |
| Do durable checkpoints reduce compaction loss? | Official mechanics and community reports make state preservation and repeated work plausible problems. | Native opaque compaction now carries prior state; no independent repository-level causal study in this registry establishes checkpoint benefit net of maintenance/context cost. | Important unresolved mechanism, but current induction and observation are too artificial or opaque for a clean experiment. |
| Is higher reasoning effort worthwhile? | IND-018 and related TTC work report benchmark gains; VEN-002 recommends intentional escalation. | Cost, latency, variance, task mix, and model implementation can erase or reverse the value. | Already conditionally answered at a broad level. A useful replication needs task interactions and substantially more power than a simple low/high comparison. |

## Maintenance rule

Review rapidly expiring Tier 2/3 entries at least quarterly and immediately when
a cited model generation, runtime, harness, pricing/caching rule, or official
page materially changes. Independent results remain historically valid for
their tested scope but become `not revalidated for current generation`; they are
never silently rewritten.
